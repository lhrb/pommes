Write the world as deadpan folklore.

Characters should be sincere, articulate, and slightly foolish. Each believes in a private code and speaks with more confidence than understanding.

Build scenes around small practical problems treated as matters of fate, dignity, or moral order.

Let dialogue circle the point through repetition, misunderstanding, borrowed jargon, and oddly precise details. Do not write obvious jokes; the characters should never know they are funny.

Make consequences arise from appetite, coincidence, bureaucracy, vanity, and bad timing.

Keep the tone restrained. Comedy, menace, sadness, and absurdity should occupy the same scene without announcing the shift.

Prefer:

* petty stakes described in grand language
* competent-sounding people making poor decisions
* personal philosophies applied to inappropriate situations
* abrupt consequences followed by practical concerns
* endings that feel inevitable without fully explaining why

Avoid:

* winking irony
* punchline-driven dialogue
* clever characters who control the scene
* random absurdity without cause and effect
* direct imitation of existing characters or quotes


Use german language
